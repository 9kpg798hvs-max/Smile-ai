# SmileFlow AI — Summary for the partners

## The problem it solves
Every evening we check on our post-op patients by hand. It works, but it's slow,
it happens on personal cell phones, none of it is recorded, and it's easy for a
worried patient to get lost in a pile of "doing fine" replies.

## What we've built
A tool that does the sending and the sorting — **but not the doctoring.** Each
evening:
1. A staff member photographs the day's schedule; the tool reads it and the
   doctor confirms the list.
2. A friendly check-in text goes out from a **practice number** (not anyone's
   personal cell).
3. Replies come back into one inbox, **sorted worst-first**, with a suggested
   response already written.
4. **A doctor approves every reply before it sends.** Patients doing well can be
   cleared in a single tap; anything urgent is flagged and pushed to the top.

The key safety feature: it reads *symptoms, not tone*, so the cheerful-sounding
text that mentions swelling still gets flagged as urgent — the exact case that's
easy to miss by hand.

## Where it stands
It's **fully built and demonstrated on pretend patients** — we've watched it run
end to end. It has not been used with a real patient yet, on purpose.

## What we're asking the group to approve
1. **Sign three standard privacy contracts** (with the AI provider, the texting
   provider, and the cloud host). Our attorney is handling these.
2. **Budget for setup**: hiring an IT contractor to install it securely, plus
   modest monthly running costs (texting, hosting, and AI usage — expected to be
   small at our volume; exact figures being confirmed).
3. **A one-doctor, one-month pilot** with real patients before we roll it to the
   whole group — so we prove it in the smallest, safest way first.

## What it changes for us
- Patients get a same-night, personal-feeling check-in from the practice.
- The worried ones surface immediately instead of hiding in the pile.
- Every conversation is recorded and off our personal phones.
- Over time we get real data on which procedures generate the most post-op
  concern — something no one else has.

## What it does *not* do
It does not diagnose, it does not text anything a doctor hasn't approved, and it
does not replace clinical judgment. It shortens the distance between a worried
patient and us; that's all.

*The software is a working prototype on test data. Nothing goes to a real patient until the contracts are signed and a doctor runs the one-month pilot.*
